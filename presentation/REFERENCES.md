# References used in the finale deck

Papers motivate a representation or test; the named project artefact supports the project
result.

1. Grinsztajn, Oyallon & Varoquaux (2022), *Why do tree-based models still outperform deep
   learning on typical tabular data?*, NeurIPS. Small-tabular baseline rationale.
2. Kim et al. (2018), *Polymer Genome*, J. Phys. Chem. C, DOI `10.1021/acs.jpcc.8b02913`.
   Polymer-aware atomic-environment descriptors.
3. Rogers & Hahn (2010), *Extended-connectivity fingerprints*, JCIM, DOI `10.1021/ci100050t`.
   Morgan fingerprint context.
4. Lundberg et al. (2020), *From local explanations to global understanding with explainable AI
   for trees*, Nature Machine Intelligence, DOI `10.1038/s42256-019-0138-9`. TreeSHAP.
5. Hooker et al. (2019), *A benchmark for interpretability methods in deep neural networks*,
   NeurIPS, arXiv:`1806.10758`. Removal/masking fidelity method.
6. Bjerrum (2017), *SMILES enumeration as data augmentation for neural network modelling of
   molecules*, arXiv:`1703.07076`. Randomized-SMILES test motivation.
7. Fox & Flory (1950), *Second-order transition temperatures and related properties of
   polystyrene*, J. Applied Physics, DOI `10.1063/1.1699435`. Tg/molecular-weight background.
8. Krogh & Vedelsby (1995), *Neural network ensembles, cross validation, and active learning*,
   NeurIPS. Error-diversity rationale for an ensemble arm.
